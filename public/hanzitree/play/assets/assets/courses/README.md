# Course content

Hanzi Tree teaches contemporary Standard Mandarin (普通话) as used in Mainland China, using simplified Chinese characters. Regional forms may occasionally be included when useful.

## Character origins

Character origins in Hanzi Tree are AI-assisted and individually reviewed. The starting etymology data comes primarily from the open-source Make Me a Hanzi dataset and from Richard Sears’ Chinese Etymology / Uncle Hanzi, whose extensive database traces characters through oracle-bone, bronze, seal-script and later forms. These source notes are then checked and interpreted using historical Chinese lexicography and modern paleographic resources, including the Shuowen Jiezi (說文解字), the Chinese University of Hong Kong’s Multi-function Chinese Character Database (漢語多功能字庫), zi.tools, and research on early Chinese writing. AI is used to bring these sources together and turn often highly technical or conflicting analyses into short explanations for learners. Where a character’s origin is uncertain or disputed, Hanzi Tree aims to say so rather than presenting a learner mnemonic as established etymology.

Keep this note in sync with `lib/character_origins_copy.dart` and the app README.

When writing lessons, grammar tips, cloze sentences, and TSV rows:

- Default to contemporary Mainland 普通话 vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation (pinyin).
- Write simplified characters as the primary form.
- Include a regional form (for example 哪儿 next to 哪里) only when it helps learners, and treat it as optional or interchangeable rather than the default.
