## Context

Learners often memorize whole glyphs without seeing the structure underneath — and then struggle when a new character appears. Decomposition is the same skill native readers use to make sense of unfamiliar writing.

Hanzi Tree treats characters as structured parts. Learners follow a skill tree of short puzzles that either assemble a character from IDS components or recognize pictographs and radicals, keeping composition and recognition as clear, distinct modes.

## Role & scope

Product concept through software implementation in Flutter: skill tree, puzzle modes, progress persistence, hints, tutorial flow, and a growing library of course content aligned with how characters are built. Bundled audio covers each of the 900+ words with male and female Chinese voices.

## Approach

The curriculum moves from early pictographs and radicals toward real vocabulary, with gated practice and competency and mastery rings. Content stays in versioned course data; modes keep assembly and recognition focused. The near-term path is Android and web builds, with store publishing still ahead.

## Outcome

A working Flutter app with foundations through early IDS and radical drills, local progress, and tutorial support. More course material exists than is wired into the tree; the product is iterating toward a shippable learner loop.
